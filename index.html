01 — The film

One night, two brothers, and a mother who isn't coming back.

Two Rats is a 2D hand-drawn animated short film, roughly 5 minutes long, set over a single foggy winter night on the streets of a city that is usually calm after dark.

Outside a housing society, a mother rat is crushed beneath the reversing wheels of a car. Her two sons are left behind on the cold concrete: an older rat who understands that they can't stay, and a younger one who doesn't understand what has happened and clings to his dead mother.

The older brother tries to pull him away. He calls, pushes and pleads. The younger one resists, weeps and watches his brother do something he can't make sense of, something that finally reaches him. He gets up. He follows.

They find shelter in a small hollow near a tree in a park. The younger rat closes his eyes. The older one stays awake beside him, watching the dark. Before dawn, he returns to the street where their mother lay. There is almost nothing left. A cat disappears around the corner. He runs back, presses close against his brother, and finally closes his eyes too.

“We wanted rats to behave like rats, not like characters performing emotion for an audience.”

The film has no dialogue. We wanted the animals to feel like animals going through something they have no words for. To understand their movement and behaviour, we reached out to people who keep pet rats. They shared videos and recordings and helped us understand how rats move, react and comfort each other. We read, watched and observed wherever we could.

02 — The world

The city doesn't stop for them.

The film takes place on one cold, foggy night. Yellow streetlights, concrete, a lot of dark.

A tarpaulin shelter on a footpath. A bonfire near the society gate where a security guard keeps himself warm. A woman visible through a window, finishing her kitchen work before the light goes off.

Nobody notices the rats, and the film doesn't point any of this out. It's just what a night like that looks like.

03 — Where it began

Where do they go after this?

The film began with something that actually happened.

Rats used to get into my home. I caught a few in a cage and released them in a nearby park, the same park that appears in the film. I watched them through the whole process. They were afraid and trembling. When I opened the cage, they simply ran.

I couldn't stop thinking about it. Where do they go after this? Did they have someone waiting for them? Could they have a family?

That thought kept growing until it became this story.

I kept it short because the budget is small. But I also didn't want to explain anything. I want you to watch Two Rats and feel what you feel.

04 — Status & budget

Where the film stands.

Two Rats is currently in development, and 15% of the film is complete. So far that is mostly key animation, and illustration is in progress for the first shot.

The film started in October 2025, and because of budget constraints it is moving at a very slow pace. Your support will help us speed things up, so the film can be completed, including post-production, within six months.

06 — Why crowdfunding?

Why crowdfunding?

This is my first short film. It's five minutes long, it's very personal to me, and I'm making it independently. A film like this is unlikely to earn its money back, and we've already put in our own savings and a lot of time.

We'll keep going either way. But with help from friends, family and anyone who connects with the story, we can finish the film properly and sooner.
